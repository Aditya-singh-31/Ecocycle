⚖️ Government Guidelines

Official Disposal Rules for Every Waste Stream

♻️ Plastic Waste

Most plastics can be recycled after rinsing. Remove caps and flatten containers when possible.

  • Disposal: Plastic Recycling Bin
  • Info: Avoid mixing with food-soiled plastics.
  • Notes: Recycling reduces ocean pollution and energy use.

🌿 Organic Waste

Food scraps, yard trimmings, and compostable materials belong in green waste collections.

  • Disposal: Compost Bin
  • Info: Keep non-compostable packaging out of organic waste.
  • Notes: Composting lowers methane emissions from landfills.

📰 Paper Waste

Reuse or recycle paper, cardboard, and paperboard. Keep material dry and free of grease.

  • Disposal: Paper Recycling Container
  • Info: Shredded paper should be contained in a bag.
  • Notes: Paper recycling conserves trees and water.

🔷 Glass Waste

Glass bottles and jars are recyclable when separated by color and cleaned.

  • Disposal: Glass Recycling Bin
  • Info: Do not include ceramics or broken glass.
  • Notes: Recycled glass saves raw materials and energy.

🔩 Metal Waste

Tins, cans, and scrap metal can be recycled. Remove food residues and labels when possible.

  • Disposal: Metal Recycling Bin
  • Info: Keep metal containers empty and clean.
  • Notes: Metal recycling supports manufacturing efficiency.

💻 E-Waste

Electronics must be collected by certified programs. Never dispose of e-waste in general trash.

  • Disposal: E-Waste Collection Center
  • Info: Remove batteries if possible before drop-off.
  • Notes: Recover valuable metals and protect ecosystems.

⚠️ Hazardous Waste

Batteries, chemicals, and solvents require special handling at hazardous waste facilities.

  • Disposal: Hazardous Waste Collection
  • Info: Do not mix incompatible substances.
  • Notes: Proper disposal prevents soil and water contamination.
